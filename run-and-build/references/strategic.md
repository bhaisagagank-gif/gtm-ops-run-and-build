# Strategic initiatives

## Validate the problem before the solution

For every candidate initiative, establish:
- **Business problem:** what's happening, who's affected, how often, current workaround.
- **Evidence:** conversations, data or reports; how many teams; measurable impact. Separate evidence from assumptions.
- **Root cause:** missing data, process, technology, ownership, visibility or automation.
- **Cost of doing nothing:** revenue, pipeline, conversion, customer experience, efficiency, risk.

## Fields every initiative carries

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| Pillar | Revenue intelligence; AI-native GTM and sales productivity; lead lifecycle and conversion; revenue data and attribution; marketing intelligence; GTM infrastructure and scalability; customer intelligence |
| Validation status | Observed → Emerging pattern → Validated → Business case ready → Approved |
| Lifecycle stage | Discover → Investigate → Validate → Business case → Approve → Plan → Build → Deploy → Measure → Optimise |
| Horizon | H1 (0–3 months), H2 (3–12 months), H3 (12–24+ months), Watchlist |
| Business owner | Who owns the outcome — don't assume ops owns it |
| Evidence | Sources supporting the problem |
| Next decision | The decision required to move forward |

## Portfolio rules

- An idea is not approved work. Approval comes from the business owner or decision-maker.
- An initiative isn't delivered until its outcome is measured against a baseline.
- Don't create duplicate initiatives for the same underlying problem — merge them.
- Keep active builds within real capacity; recommend pausing others when something new qualifies.
- Stop initiatives that no longer justify their investment, regardless of time already spent.
- Recommend the smallest pilot that can validate the business case.

## Recommendation options

Invest now · Validate further · Defer · Reject — always with the reason and what it would displace.

## Business case template

1. Initiative name
2. Executive summary
3. Business problem and root cause
4. Supporting evidence
5. Current state
6. Proposed future state
7. Expected impact (label estimates and assumptions)
8. Solution architecture (data, integrations, automation, AI, human approval points, governance)
9. Implementation complexity
10. Investment (effort, people, external cost, maintenance, dependencies)
11. Success metrics with baselines
12. Stakeholders (sponsor, business owner, technical owner, decision-makers)
13. Risks and dependencies
14. Recommendation
